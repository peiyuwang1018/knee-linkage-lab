Chain & idler workbench

Version 0.9 · mean pitch-path geometry, fixed idlers and ideal chain forces.

Teeth follow the ratio limit

The minimum reduction ratio is fixed at 1. Set Ratio max between 1 and 2. Output teeth are constrained to z₁ ≤ z₂ ≤ floor(z₁ × ratio_max). Changing the input teeth or ratio limit adjusts the output teeth into that interval. For 19 input teeth and ratio_max = 1.55, the largest output is 29 teeth (ratio 1.526), not 30. Imported older configurations are migrated to the same rule.

Input tip diameter defaults to a 100 mm limit. This is an estimated tooth-tip envelope, not the full housing or chain envelope. Supplier CAD governs final sizing.

The readouts next to input, output and idler teeth show PCD · mm: the geometric pitch diameter d=p/sin(π/z), updated with pitch and tooth count. Hover for three decimals. PCD is distinct from tooth-tip diameter and the mean transmission diameter pz/π.

Chain dimensions

Below the chain selector, p is pitch, d₁ is roller/bush diameter and b₁ is the clear spacing between inner plates. Chain data opens a table for all chain types with plate height, overall pin width, allowable working load and manufacturer links.

Sprocket tooth width must be smaller than b₁ with the supplier's specified axial clearance. Do not confuse it with overall chain width. The 25RB and 35RB are bush chains, without separate rollers. Custom geometry exposes inner spacing and total width independently.

Link-count range and idlers

  1. Set Chain pitches min/max; one pitch is one link. The selected N must be even. Scan enumerates every routable even count in the range.
  2. Choose Single · span B or Mirrored pair · A/B. Both act from outside toward the drive centerline. Span labels stay fixed; B is not guaranteed to remain slack when torque reverses.
  3. Set idler teeth, maximum inward travel and planar clearance. Idlers use the same chain pitch and ideal reverse-bend contact.
  4. Auto x + travel tries x/C from 0.20 to 0.80 in 0.05 increments, then solves inward displacement to match Np. It selects the feasible position with the smallest travel. Fixed x solves travel at the chosen x/C.
  5. Fit even links loads the first fitting even N in your range. Changing N recomputes idler locations and reactions.

The origin is the input wheel, +x points to the output wheel, and +y points toward span A. The mirrored pair has the same x and opposite y coordinates, with equal inward travel. Position readouts report local x/y in mm. Dashed guides run from initial contact centers to the fitted centers; green arrows show chain forces on the idlers.

More links require more deflection. They are not unlimited: the route must respect travel, drive-wheel wrap, wheel envelopes, nonadjacent span/wheel clearance and span crossing/proximity checks. The default 19/38-tooth 35RB pair with 300 mm centers and 15-tooth idlers fits 92–94 pitches with a mirrored pair, or 92–98 with a single idler, under the default clearance/travel limits. No solution shows only the labeled two-wheel reference path, with no fabricated chain forces. No-idler mode accepts a mean-length mismatch of at most 0.1 mm.

Path calculation

The mean transmission radius is R = pz/(2π). The geometric pitch diameter is separately d = p/sin(π/z), with an upper tooth-tip estimate dₐ ≈ d + 1.25p − d₁.

Each route comprises exact common tangents of the mean circles and circular wrap arcs. Main sprockets and outside idlers have opposite wrap directions. Signed radii select the appropriate internal or external tangent. At each candidate x, inward travel is solved by bisection until the complete path equals Np. This is not a polygonal drawing shortcut.

The two-wheel reference length is 2√(C²−ΔR²)+π(R₁+R₂)+2ΔR asin(ΔR/C). The old added-path allowance is retained only for file compatibility; it no longer admits an unsolved route.

Loads, torque and support reactions

The foot is fixed and the hip is vertically above it. Constant loading uses Fz = m × share × g × κ. Acceleration loading uses a hip-lumped mass and g + ḧ; link inertia and impacts are omitted. Nonpositive ground force invalidates contact. Prescribed knee torque does not determine foot force or total knee support.

ΔT = τk/R₂
TA = Tslack + max(ΔT,0)
TB = Tslack + max(−ΔT,0)

The slack tension is prescribed, not generated by a spring model. A chain cannot push. For each wheel, using route travel directions at entry and exit, Fwheel = Tout·uout − Tin·uin. An ideal free idler has equal incoming/outgoing tension but a nonzero force due to the direction change. Input, output and all idler forces balance globally; input and output wheel loads generally differ after idlers are added.

Output chain support is the output wheel's chain resultant. Input chain support reports the input wheel. Largest idler support is the largest individual idler load. Total knee support also balances foot force. These are shaft resultants, not individual bearing loads; bearing spacing and overhang are still needed.

Reduction is i=z₂/z₁ and relative module speed is i·q̇. Module torque is τk/(iη) when driving and ητk/i when backdriven; zero speed uses the conservative driving expression. Losses are lumped at the module; chain reactions remain ideal. Defaults of 55 N·m / 120 rpm refer to the output of the AKE90-8 KV35 internal gearbox.

Reading the chain load check

The panel above the chart separates Now · max(A, B) from the highest individual span tension over the complete outward and return cycle. It compares that peak with Working limit = effective allowable working load / chain load factor. Peak utilization is peak tension divided by this working limit; above 100% shows a red Over limit status. The tension plot always includes the working limit, with a pale red area above it.

For 35RB, the catalog maximum allowable working load is 480 lbf, about 2135 N (rechecked 17 November 2026). With a catalog multiplier of 1 and a load factor of 1.25, the working limit is 1708 N. A 2000 N tension gives 2500 N design load and 117.1% utilization, exceeding the configured limit. This is a working-load screening margin, not a breaking-strength safety factor or a fatigue-life prediction.

Missing allowable ratings show Rating unknown. An unsolved route or no valid force samples shows No force data. A partial cycle cannot claim a complete pass. Module torque coverage remains a separate check.

Screening and scans

Scan enumerates integer teeth and even pitches, fitting the selected idler layout for each candidate. Runtime can reach tens of seconds. Load a candidate to inspect its geometry, current values and cycle peaks. Chain rating is compared against peak span tension multiplied by the chain load factor. Missing allowable working load stays Unrated; breaking strength is never substituted. An override applies only to the selected product.

Pareto ranking minimizes output tip diameter, chain width and peak module torque among complete screen passes. It does not minimize idler load. Equal teeth with different N can share the front but have different idler positions and shaft reactions. One-way plots retain differing return values as dashed curves. Green coverage concerns rated module torque over the requested knee-angle range in both directions; speed is checked separately.

Sprocket and idler selection

ISO 606 covers matched chain/sprocket dimensions and tolerances. Equal pitch alone does not establish compatibility; roller/bush diameter, tooth profile and axial clearance matter.

  • Renold's design guide favors at least 19 teeth on the small drive wheel. Treat smaller wheels as a specific design tradeoff, especially under impact.
  • Tsubaki recommends at least three engaged idler teeth. Estimate wrap coverage as zᵢβᵢ/360°; this is not a discrete contact count. Three pitches require about 98.2° for an 11-tooth idler.
  • Renold's installation guide (printed p.205) generally favors idlers no smaller than the smallest drive sprocket, with at least four pitches of free chain to the nearest sprocket.
  • For reversing torque, A/B swap tight/slack roles. A fixed adjustable idler and a spring tensioner are different models. Check bidirectional loads, spring deflection and stops before adopting a tensioner.
  • Ideal idler shaft load is 2T sin(β/2); bearing loads also depend on spacing and overhang. Avoid excessive preload.

Screening scope: the current solver checks main-wheel wrap and planar clearance, but does not enforce the three-tooth idler-engagement or four-pitch free-span recommendations. A closed route can still be unsuitable. With 35RB, 19/33 teeth, N=92, mirrored 11-tooth idlers and other defaults, the model gives only about 45.1° wrap (1.38 tooth pitches) per idler.

What remains to verify

This is a fixed-idler planar layout, not a spring tensioner simulation. Check supplier approval for reverse bending, tooth profile, joining links and actual idler geometry. Current clearance checks exclude hubs, bearings, housing and 3D leg collisions. Discrete engagement, backlash, elasticity, friction, wear and eccentricity are not solved, so the chordal speed index is not a predicted torque ripple. A screen pass is a preliminary design filter, not a fatigue, thermal or manufacturing qualification.

Save/Open preserves configurations; CSV includes the fitted route and force history; SVG exports the drawing. The Python suite includes tangent orthogonality, path closure, force/moment balance and an independent idler virtual-work check using the numerical path-length derivative.
